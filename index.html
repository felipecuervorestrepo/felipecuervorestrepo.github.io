01 / Research

Thinking between traditions

My research brings metaphysical questions into conversation with Buddhist, Japanese, and Latin American philosophical traditions. My work explores animism, the boundaries of the subject, the structure of time, and the relations between parts and wholes.

I hold doctorates from Kyoto University and Universidad de los Andes. At the Kyoto Institute of Philosophy, I work on comparative philosophy and animism, alongside academic cooperation with Latin America.

Teaching & service

University teaching

I teach Philosophy of Science at Universidad El Bosque and in the Creative Writing program at Universidad Central, where my courses cover the development of thought, semiotics, and pragmatics.

Previously Assistant Professor of Philosophy at Universidad El Bosque (2017–2022), teaching logic, epistemology, philosophy of language, mind, action, and science, and supervising undergraduate theses.
